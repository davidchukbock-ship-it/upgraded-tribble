How it all works, in plain words

No experience with international hiring is needed. This page explains who does what, what the terms mean, and what to expect from your first message to the first working day.

Draft: statements about cost, timing and responsibilities must be confirmed by GCN before publication.

The idea in one minute

You need people for a job: a harvest, a warehouse, a building site, a restaurant season. Finding, checking and preparing workers abroad takes time and local knowledge.

GCN does this groundwork. We find candidates in countries with available workers, check their skills, prepare them for the role and organize the practical side.

Legal steps, such as work permits and employment contracts, always follow the rules of the country where the work happens, and are handled by licensed parties where the law requires it.

Who is who

Government authorities decide on work permits. Nobody else, including GCN, can promise their decision.

You, the employer

Describe the job, choose a model and manage the daily work on your site.

GCN

Finds and prepares candidates, builds the team, coordinates each step and stays in touch.

Authorized partners and licensed employers

Local organizations that handle what only a licensed party may do, such as employment or recruitment in a given country.

The worker

Does the job, follows your safety rules and the local law.

Key terms, explained

You will meet these words when you work with us. Here is what they mean.

Staffing
We find and prepare people; you hire and employ them directly.
Outstaffing
People work for your business day to day, while a licensed employer is formally responsible for contracts, payroll and administration. Only where local law allows it.
Workforce team
A group of workers formed for one job or one site.
Seasonal team
Needed for a few months, such as a harvest or a hotel high season.
Project-based team
Needed until a specific piece of work is finished, such as one stage of construction.
Long-term team
Ongoing roles with no fixed end date.
Licensed employer
A company that the law allows to employ workers, or to provide them to other businesses, in a given country.
Work authorization
Government permission to work in a country. Issued by the authorities, not by GCN or by the employer.
Talent sourcing
Searching for, contacting and shortlisting candidates.
Onboarding
The first days: documents, safety briefing, introduction to the site and the tasks.
Referral fee
A payment to a partner who introduces a client company or a candidate.

Staffing or outstaffing?

The two models differ mainly in who is the formal employer. Which one is available depends on the country.

From first message to first working day

Six steps. At each one it is clear what you do and what we do.

  1. 01

    First message

    You: Tell us the roles, how many people, where and when.

    We: We usually reply within one business day and ask clarifying questions.

  2. 02

    Plan

    You: Confirm the team format and the timeline.

    We: We propose a model and market, and say clearly what is active, through partners or planned.

  3. 03

    Search and checks

    You: Approve the requirements; interview candidates if you wish.

    We: We find candidates, check their skills and make a shortlist.

  4. 04

    Preparation and paperwork

    You: Sign the agreements for the chosen model.

    We: We prepare people for the role and coordinate documents with licensed partners. Timing also depends on the authorities.

  5. 05

    Arrival and start

    You: Prepare the workplace, the safety briefing and housing if you provide it.

    We: We organize the logistics and coordinate the arrival.

  6. 06

    Work and support

    You: Manage the daily work as you do with your own staff.

    We: We stay in touch and adjust the team size as your needs change.

What to prepare before you write to us

Do not worry if you do not know all the answers. We fill the gaps together in the first conversation.

  • Which jobs you need filled, and how many people for each.
  • Where the work is: country, state or province, and city.
  • When people should start and for how long.
  • Skills, language level and any certificates the job needs.
  • Working conditions: hours, pay range, and housing or transport if you provide them.
  • Who on your side will be our contact.

What no one can promise you

Permits are decided by governments

We plan around the requirements of each country, but nobody can guarantee a visa or a permit.

Timing depends on the country and the role

After the first conversation we give a realistic plan instead of a promise that sounds good.

We are not a law firm

We do not give legal or immigration advice. For your own case, talk to a qualified lawyer.

Status differs by market

Every market is marked as current service, through authorized partners, or planned.

Be careful with any provider that:

  • promises a guaranteed visa or permit
  • avoids putting terms in writing
  • cannot say who the formal employer is
  • asks workers for large payments up front

Beginner questions

Ask our Telegram bot

Do I need a company in the country where the workers come from?

No. GCN and its partners handle the work on that side.

Do I need experience with hiring abroad?

No. In the first conversation we explain the steps for your case.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the team format, the number of people, the market and the legal model. After your brief we give a written breakdown.

How long until the team arrives?

It depends on the country, the role and how fast the authorities process documents. We give a realistic timeline after the first conversation.

Who is responsible for the workers on site?

You direct the daily work and safety on your site. Contracts and payroll belong to whoever is the formal employer under the chosen model, as written in the agreement.

Can I interview candidates first?

Usually yes. Say so in your request and we will arrange it where the process allows.

I am a candidate or a partner. What do I do?

Write to our Telegram bot. It guides you to the right next step.
